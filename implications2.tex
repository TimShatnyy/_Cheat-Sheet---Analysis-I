Bolzano--Weierstrass on $[a,b]$ &
A sequence $(x_n)\subseteq[a,b]$ is bounded. By Bolzano--Weierstrass,
it has a convergent subsequence
$
x_{n_k}\to x_0.
$
Since
$
a\le x_{n_k}\le b
$
for all $k$, order preservation under limits yields
$
a\le x_0\le b.
$
Thus $x_0\in[a,b]$.
\\

\midrule

Continuous on compact $\Rightarrow$ uniformly continuous &
Assume not. Then some $\varepsilon_0>0$ and sequences
$x_n,y_n\in[a,b]$ satisfy
$
|x_n-y_n|<1/n
$
but
$
|f(x_n)-f(y_n)|\ge\varepsilon_0.
$
Choose a subsequence $x_{n_k}\to x^*\in[a,b]$. Since
$
|x_{n_k}-y_{n_k}|\to0,
$
also $y_{n_k}\to x^*$. Continuity gives
$
f(x_{n_k}),f(y_{n_k})\to f(x^*),
$
hence
$
|f(x_{n_k})-f(y_{n_k})|\to0,
$
a contradiction.
\\

\midrule

Intermediate value theorem &
Assume $f(a)\le c\le f(b)$ and set
$
X:=\{x\in[a,b]\mid f(x)\le c\},
\qquad
x^*:=\sup X.
$
Choose $x_n\in X$ with $x_n\to x^*$; continuity gives
$
f(x^*)\le c.
$
If $f(x^*)<c$, continuity yields $\delta>0$ such that
$
f(y)<c
$
for $|y-x^*|<\delta$, producing $y>x^*$ in $X$, contradicting the
definition of $x^*$. Hence
$
f(x^*)=c.
$
\\

\midrule

Cauchy criterion for series &
Let
$
s_n:=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_k.
$
Then
$
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_k
$
converges iff $(s_n)$ converges, iff $(s_n)$ is Cauchy. Since
$
s_n-s_m=\sum_{k=m+1}^{n}a_k,
$
this is equivalent to
$
\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists N\
\forall n\ge m\ge N:
\left|\sum_{k=m+1}^{n}a_k\right|<\varepsilon.
$
\\

\midrule

Absolute convergence $\Rightarrow$ convergence &
Assume
$
\sum |a_n|
$
converges. By the Cauchy criterion, for large $n\ge m$,
$
\sum_{k=m+1}^{n}|a_k|<\varepsilon.
$
Therefore
$
\left|\sum_{k=m+1}^{n}a_k\right|
\le
\sum_{k=m+1}^{n}|a_k|
<\varepsilon.
$
Thus $\sum a_n$ is Cauchy and converges. Moreover,
$
\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_k\right|
\le
\sum_{k=0}^{n}|a_k|;
$
passing to the limit gives
$
\left|\sum a_k\right|\le\sum|a_k|.
$
\\

\midrule

Leibniz criterion &
Let
$
s_n:=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^ka_k
$
with $a_n\downarrow0$. Then
$
s_{2n+2}-s_{2n}=-a_{2n+1}+a_{2n+2}\le0,
$
so $(s_{2n})$ decreases, while
$
s_{2n+3}-s_{2n+1}=a_{2n+2}-a_{2n+3}\ge0,
$
so $(s_{2n+1})$ increases. Also
$
s_{2n+1}\le s_{2n}
$
and
$
s_{2n}-s_{2n+1}=a_{2n+1}\to0.
$
Thus both subsequences converge to the same limit, hence $(s_n)$ converges.
\\

\midrule

Rearrangement of an absolutely convergent series &
Let $\varphi:\mathbb N_0\to\mathbb N_0$ be bijective and
$
\sum|a_n|<\infty.
$
Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose $N$ such that
$
\sum_{n>N}|a_n|<\varepsilon.
$
Choose $M$ so that
$
\{0,\ldots,N\}
\subseteq
\{\varphi(0),\ldots,\varphi(M)\}.
$
For $m\ge M$, the difference between
$
\sum_{k=0}^{m}a_{\varphi(k)}
$
and
$
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_n
$
contains only terms with index $>N$, so its absolute value is
$<\varepsilon$. Hence the rearranged series has the same sum; applying
the same argument to $|a_n|$ gives absolute convergence.
\\

\textbf{\large Solutions I}\par

\midrule

Taylor polynomial &
Use
$T_n f(x;x_0)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}
\frac{f^{(k)}(x_0)}{k!}(x-x_0)^k$.
Here $x_0=-1$ and
$f(-1)=f'(-1)=f'''(-1)=0$, $f''(-1)=2$.
Thus
$T_2f(x;-1)=\frac{2}{2!}(x+1)^2=(x+1)^2$
and
$T_3f(x;-1)=(x+1)^2$.
Hence
$f(-0.99)\approx T_2f(-0.99;-1)=(0.01)^2=10^{-4}$.
\\

\midrule

$x\sin(1/x)$ at $0$ &
Let $f(0)=0$ and $f(x)=x\sin(1/x)$ for $x\neq0$.
The difference quotient is
$\frac{f(h)-f(0)}h=\sin(1/h)$.
Choose $h_n=2/(\pi n)\to0$; then
$\sin(1/h_n)=\sin(\pi n/2)$ does not converge.
Hence $f$ is not differentiable at $0$.
However, $|f(x)|\le|x|\to0$, so $f$ is continuous at $0$.
\\

\midrule

$x^2\sin(1/x)$ at $0$ &
Let $f(0)=0$ and $f(x)=x^2\sin(1/x)$ for $x\neq0$.
Then
$\frac{f(h)-f(0)}h=h\sin(1/h)$ and
$|h\sin(1/h)|\le|h|\to0$.
By the squeeze theorem,
$\boxed{f'(0)=0}$.
\\

\midrule

Piecewise corner &
For
$g(x)=e^{-x}$ if $x>0$ and $g(x)=1$ if $x\le0$,
$g$ is continuous at $0$, but
$g'_+(0)=\lim_{h\to0^+}\frac{e^{-h}-1}{h}=-1$
and
$g'_-(0)=\lim_{h\to0^-}\frac{1-1}{h}=0$.
Thus $\boxed{g\text{ is not differentiable at }0}$.
\\

\midrule

Vertical tangent &
For $f(x)=\sqrt[3]{x}$,
$\frac{f(x)-f(0)}x=x^{-2/3}\to+\infty$ as $x\to0$.
Thus no finite derivative exists at $0$; geometrically the tangent is
vertical.
\\

\midrule

Rational--irrational function &
Let
$f(x)=x$ for $x\in\mathbb Q$ and
$f(x)=0$ for $x\notin\mathbb Q$.
At $0$, $|f(x)|\le|x|\to0$, so $f$ is continuous.
For $x_0\neq0$, choose rational $r_n\to x_0$ and irrational
$s_n\to x_0$. Then
$f(r_n)\to x_0$ but $f(s_n)\to0$.
Hence $\boxed{f\text{ is continuous exactly at }0}$.
\\

\midrule

Power-series radius &
For $\sum a_nz^n$,
$R=\bigl(\limsup_{n\to\infty}|a_n|^{1/n}\bigr)^{-1}$,
with $1/0=\infty$.
Thus $|z|<R$ gives absolute convergence and $|z|>R$ divergence.
Examples:
$a_n=(-1)^n/(2n+1)$ gives $\limsup|a_n|^{1/n}=1$, so $R=1$;
$a_n=e^{in}/(4n)!$ gives limit $0$, so $R=\infty$;
coefficients of order $1/k^2$ on even indices give $R=1$.
\\

\midrule

Rationalizing a root &
For $a_n\to0$,
$\frac{\sqrt{1+a_n}-1}{a_n}
\cdot
\frac{\sqrt{1+a_n}+1}{\sqrt{1+a_n}+1}
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+a_n}+1}$.
Since $\sqrt{1+a_n}\to1$,
$\boxed{\frac{\sqrt{1+a_n}-1}{a_n}\to\frac12}$.
\\

\midrule

Spring oscillator &
At equilibrium,
$
-mg+ks_0=0
\Rightarrow
s_0=\frac{mg}{k}.
$
Let $x(t)$ be the displacement from equilibrium. Then Newton's law gives
$
m\ddot{x}+kx=0,
\qquad
\ddot{x}+\omega^2x=0,
\qquad
\omega=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}.
$
The characteristic roots are
$
\lambda=\pm i\omega,
$
hence
$
x(t)=C_1\sin(\omega t)+C_2\cos(\omega t).
$
For
$
x(0)=x_0,
\quad
\dot{x}(0)=v_0,
$
$
\boxed{
x(t)=\frac{v_0}{\omega}\sin(\omega t)+x_0\cos(\omega t)
}.
$
Moreover,
$
T=\frac{2\pi}{\omega},
\
\nu=\frac{\omega}{2\pi},
\
A=\sqrt{x_0^2+\frac{v_0^2}{\omega^2}}.
$
The energy
$
E(t)=\frac{m}{2}\dot{x}(t)^2+\frac{k}{2}x(t)^2
$
is conserved because
$
E'(t)
=
\dot{x}(t)\bigl(m\ddot{x}(t)+kx(t)\bigr)
=
0.
$
Thus
$
\boxed{
E(t)=E(0)=\frac{m}{2}v_0^2+\frac{k}{2}x_0^2
}.
$
\\

Subsequence criterion &
If $a_n\to L$ and $b_k=a_{n_k}$ is a subsequence, then
$n_k\ge k$.
For $\varepsilon>0$, choose $N$ with
$n\ge N\Rightarrow|a_n-L|<\varepsilon$.
For $k\ge N$,
$n_k\ge k\ge N$, hence
$|b_k-L|=|a_{n_k}-L|<\varepsilon$.
Thus $\boxed{b_k\to L}$.
\\

\midrule

Dominant exponential &
For
$\frac{(-2024)^n+(-2025)^n}
{(-2024)^{n+1}-(-2025)^n}$,
divide numerator and denominator by $(-2025)^n$:
$\frac{(2024/2025)^n+1}
{(2024/2025)^n(-2024)-1}$.
Since $(2024/2025)^n\to0$, the limit is
$\boxed{-1}$.
\\

\midrule

Root of exponential sum &
Since
$17^n\le5^n+11^n+17^n\le3\cdot17^n$,
taking $n$-th roots gives
$17\le\sqrt[n]{5^n+11^n+17^n}
\le17\sqrt[n]{3}$.
As $\sqrt[n]{3}\to1$, the squeeze theorem yields
$\boxed{\sqrt[n]{5^n+11^n+17^n}\to17}$.
\\

\midrule

$n$-th root of a constant &
For $a>1$, set $b_n=\sqrt[n]{a}-1\ge0$.
Bernoulli gives
$1+nb_n\le(1+b_n)^n=a$,
so $0\le b_n\le(a-1)/n\to0$.
Hence $\sqrt[n]{a}\to1$.
For $0<a<1$, apply the result to $1/a>1$ and take reciprocals.
Thus $\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}\sqrt[n]{a}=1}$ for every $a>0$.
\\

\midrule

Polynomial roots &
For
$p(x)=x^3-4x^2+9x-10$, testing gives $p(2)=0$.
Polynomial division yields
$p(x)=(x-2)(x^2-2x+5)$.
Since
$x^2-2x+5=(x-1)^2+4
=(x-1-2i)(x-1+2i)$,
the roots are
$\boxed{2,\ 1+2i,\ 1-2i}$.
\\

\midrule

Complex $n$-th roots &
Write $w=re^{i\varphi}$. The solutions of $z^n=w$ are
$z_k=r^{1/n}e^{i(\varphi+2\pi k)/n}$,
$k=0,\ldots,n-1$.
For
$64+64\sqrt3\,i=128e^{i\pi/3}$ and $n=7$,
$\boxed{z_k=2e^{i(\pi/21+2\pi k/7)}}$,
$k=0,\ldots,6$.
\\

\midrule

Trig identity via Euler &
Using
$\sin x=\frac{e^{ix}-e^{-ix}}{2i}$ and
$\cos x=\frac{e^{ix}+e^{-ix}}2$,
expand
$\sin x\cos y+\sin y\cos x$.
After cancellation,
$\frac{e^{i(x+y)}-e^{-i(x+y)}}{2i}
=\sin(x+y)$.
Hence
$\boxed{\sin(x+y)=\sin x\cos y+\cos x\sin y}$.
\\

\midrule

Affine supremum &
Let $M=\sup S$, $P=\sup\{ax+b:x\in S\}$ and $a>0$.
For $x\in S$,
$x\le M\Rightarrow ax+b\le aM+b$,
so $P\le aM+b$.
Also,
$ax+b\le P\Rightarrow x\le(P-b)/a$;
thus $(P-b)/a$ is an upper bound of $S$, so
$M\le(P-b)/a$ and $aM+b\le P$.
Therefore
$\boxed{\sup_{x\in S}(ax+b)=a\sup_{x\in S}x+b}$.
\\

\midrule

Variable integral bound &
Use
$
\frac{d}{dx}\int_{g(x)}^{b}f(t)\,dt
=
-f(g(x))g'(x).
$
Here
$
f(t)=\sin^2(t)\cos^2(t),
\quad
g(x)=x^2.
$
Therefore
$
\boxed{
G'(x)
=
-2x\sin^2(x^2)\cos^2(x^2)
}.
$
\\

\midrule

Mixing tank &
Use
$
\text{rate of change}
=
\text{inflow}-\text{outflow}.
$
The salt inflow is
$
12\cdot0.125=1.5\ \mathrm{kg/min}.
$
Since the volume is constantly $400\,\mathrm l$, the tank concentration is
$
\frac{c(t)}{400}\ \mathrm{kg/l}.
$
Thus the salt outflow is
$
12\cdot\frac{c(t)}{400}
=
0.03c(t)\ \mathrm{kg/min}.
$
Therefore
$
\boxed{
c'(t)=1.5-0.03c(t),
\qquad
c(0)=100
}.
$
\\

\midrule

Population model &
Let $t$ be the number of years after 2010 and measure population in billions.
The death rate is linear with
$
S(0)=0.057,
\quad
S(30)=0.080.
$
Hence
$
S(t)
=
0.057+\frac{0.080-0.057}{30}t
=
0.057+\frac{23}{30000}t.
$
The birth rate is
$
G(t)=0.140.
$
Therefore
$
P'(t)=G(t)-S(t)
=
0.083-\frac{23}{30000}t,
\qquad
P(0)=6.9.
$
Integration gives
$
P(t)
=
6.9+0.083t-\frac{23}{60000}t^2.
$
For 2050, $t=40$, so
$
P(40)
=
6.9+0.083\cdot40-\frac{23}{60000}\cdot40^2
\approx9.61.
$
Thus
$
\boxed{P(2050)\approx9.61\text{ billion}}.
$
\\

\midrule

Complex polynomial roots &
Factor out the common factor:
$
p(z)
=
2z^3+(2-4i)z^2-(6+2i)z
=
2z\bigl(z^2+(1-2i)z-(3+i)\bigr).
$
Hence $z_0=0$. For the quadratic factor,
$
z_{1,2}
=
\frac{-(1-2i)\pm
\sqrt{(1-2i)^2+4(3+i)}}{2}.
$
Since
$
(1-2i)^2+4(3+i)=9,
$
we get
$
z_{1,2}
=
\frac{-1+2i\pm3}{2}.
$
Thus
$
z_1=1+i,
\qquad
z_2=-2+i.
$
Therefore
$
\boxed{
p(z)=2z(z-1-i)(z+2-i)
}
$
with zeros
$
\boxed{0,\ 1+i,\ -2+i}.
$
\\

\midrule

Optimal viewing distance &
For an object extending from height $a$ to $b$ with $0<a<b$, the viewing angle at distance $d>0$ is
$
\vartheta(d)
=
\arctan\!\left(\frac{b}{d}\right)
-
\arctan\!\left(\frac{a}{d}\right).
$
Then
$
\vartheta'(d)
=
-\frac{b}{d^2+b^2}
+
\frac{a}{d^2+a^2}.
$
Setting $\vartheta'(d)=0$ gives
$
a(d^2+b^2)=b(d^2+a^2)
\Longleftrightarrow
d^2=ab.
$
Since $\vartheta(d)\to0$ for $d\to0^+$ and $d\to\infty$, this critical point is the global maximum. Hence
$
\boxed{d=\sqrt{ab}}.
$
For $a=46$, $b=92$:
$
\boxed{d=\sqrt{46\cdot92}=46\sqrt2\approx65.05\ \mathrm m}.
$
\\

\midrule

Differentiability of $|x|^3$ &
Write
$
f(x)=|x|^3=
\begin{cases}
x^3,&x\ge0,\\
-x^3,&x<0.
\end{cases}
$
For $x\neq0$, both branches are differentiable. At $x=0$,
$
f'_+(0)
=
\lim_{h\to0^+}\frac{h^3}{h}
=
\lim_{h\to0^+}h^2
=
0,
$
and
$
f'_-(0)
=
\lim_{h\to0^-}\frac{-h^3}{h}
=
\lim_{h\to0^-}(-h^2)
=
0.
$
Since the one-sided derivatives agree,
$
\boxed{f\text{ is differentiable at }0,\qquad f'(0)=0}.
$
\\

\midrule

Tangent to integral function &
For
$
g(x)=\int_{1}^{e^x}\cos(\pi t^2)\,dt,
$
the tangent at $x_0=0$ is
$
y=g(0)+g'(0)x.
$
First,
$
g(0)=\int_1^1\cos(\pi t^2)\,dt=0.
$
By FTC + chain rule,
$
g'(x)
=
\cos\!\left(\pi(e^x)^2\right)e^x
=
e^x\cos(\pi e^{2x}).
$
Thus
$
g'(0)=\cos(\pi)=-1.
$
Hence
$
\boxed{y=-x}.
$
\\
